Findings

Six numbers. Each one changed what shipped.

Most of my best work started as a number that didn’t add up.

  1. 191°Azimuth error on one pass

    The predicted pass pointed the dish almost the opposite way. Gpredict’s bundled database was still carrying a METEOR-M2 element set from 2018. Now every tracked satellite is refreshed from SatNOGS before the tracker ever sees it.

    Ground station

  2. 1.000Spearman, “fused” model vs. XGBoost alone

    Our capstone’s multimodal model ranked every sample exactly the way its XGBoost branch did. Running the saved CNN as a black box showed why: it returned a byte-identical vector for every input it was given.

    Wildfire

  3. 20→56%Positive rate, after one join

    A left join copied each weather row once per satellite fire detection, and the headline accuracy rose with it. De-duplicated, the model scores 89.9% against an 85.0% baseline, with ROC-AUC 0.907. A smaller claim, and one I can defend.

    Wildfire

  4. 1 dayLaunch-date error on two satellites

    Cross-checking all 47 tracked satellites between SatNOGS and CelesTrak turned up two Chinese launches logged in Beijing time instead of UTC, and seven missing dates. Launch dates now come from CelesTrak, names and frequencies from SatNOGS, and neither is trusted outside its strength.

    Ground station

  5. 0.001°Agreement, three orbit calculations

    The dashboard, Gpredict, and a second SGP4 implementation I wrote with the coordinate transform done by hand all land within a thousandth of a degree. Getting there meant matching the station’s coordinates to the fourth decimal place.

    Ground station

  6. 4,056/4,056Test clips scored

    My Urdu speech model was evaluated on the entire Common Voice 17 test split, not a sample of it: 34.6% word error rate, 11.5% character error rate. The model is public, so anyone can re-run the number.

    Urdu ASR

PES University Ground Station

Software for a 3.7-metre S-band dish: fetch the orbits, point the antenna, and put everything the operator needs on one screen.

The problem

The station ran on three programs that didn’t talk to each other — one for orbital data, one for pass prediction, one for the spectrum. During an eight-minute pass, the operator reconciled them in their head. And when I started, there was no rotator to test against.

What I built

A pipeline that pulls the SatNOGS database, matches 21 requested satellites despite inconsistent naming, and writes one element file each, plus a report of what didn’t match. A tracking engine that streams azimuth and elevation to Hamlib’s rotctld: it never commands the dish below 25°, never winds the azimuth cable through its stop at north — it flips the dish over the top instead — and pre-positions before each rise, because elevation only moves at 2° a second.

Then a one-screen dashboard: SGP4 propagated locally for 47 satellites, a merged 72-hour pass list, Doppler retuning (about ±50 kHz across an S-band pass), and a Welch-averaged spectrum straight off a HackRF One, with the receiver’s own oscillator spike cut by 57 dB.

What I checked

I wrote a protocol-level rotator simulator before the controller, so the whole loop ran end to end with no hardware. (I enjoyed the simulator more than the controller.) The orbital maths agrees with Gpredict and with a hand-derived second implementation to 0.001°.

Wildfire Detection & Prevention

Daily fire risk for the Karnataka–Kerala–Tamil Nadu forest belt, from six years of NASA climate data and satellite fire detections.

My part

The upstream risk features, the hotspot heatmaps and Top-5 output, and blind-test validation of the finished model.

What I built

Five NASA POWER variables — humidity, rainfall, soil moisture, wind speed and direction — merged into one 99,954-row table on latitude, longitude and day of year. 119,623 raw VIIRS fire detections turned into 104,374 clean labels, snapped onto the 0.5° × 0.625° climate grid so fires and weather actually line up. Then I replaced min-max risk scores with fire behaviour: a sigmoid on humidity for flashover, wind scaled quadratically, a hinge at the soil wilting point, and a cut-off after heavy rain.

What I checked

Validation was mine, so I ran the saved models as black boxes. The CNN returned the same vector for every input, and 94% of its inputs were all-zero patches — only 4 of 54 grid cells fell inside the terrain rasters. A left join had lifted the positive rate from 20% to 56%. I re-measured on clean data and brought the team numbers that hold up.

Urdu Speech Recognition

Fine-tuning a 300-million-parameter speech model to transcribe Urdu, on a free GPU that switches off after twelve hours.

What I built

wav2vec2-xls-r-300m with a CTC head, trained on Common Voice 17 Urdu. Mozilla had emptied the official dataset repositories in October 2025, so the pipeline runs off a community mirror. The model is on Hugging Face with a Gradio demo anyone can talk to.

What I checked

The first runs never finished. Tracing where the twelve hours went showed that full-test-set evaluation every 400 steps was eating most of it — and that Kaggle’s P100 wasn’t supported by the installed PyTorch at all. Dual T4s and rationed evaluation got a usable checkpoint at step 6,400, which I then scored on every one of the 4,056 test clips.

Job Hunt Command Center

What I built

A scheduled crawler over 166 verified public applicant-tracking boards — about 13,000 postings a run — that scores each role against my profile and prepares the application. Deduplication had to learn that one job description posted in two cities is two jobs, not one.

What I checked

The line it won’t cross is written as a test, not a promise. It fills forms in; it never submits them. Some questions are never answered by a machine, and any profile field I haven’t confirmed is refused rather than guessed.
